# Packaging: Titles & Thumbnails

Compiled from a vidIQ AI coach session (Sept 2026) and the five-chapters packaging work (Aug 2026). See `channel.md` for the raw channel stats and keyword numbers this was built from.

---

## Channel-level direction

- Channel purpose: shadow a marketing career, showing campaigns and branding work for firearms and blue-collar companies. Wants strong themes with clickable titles and thumbnails.
- Direction shift (Aug 2026): entertainment first, education second. Teaching-only channels get watched only until the question is answered, then dropped. The channel should be fun marketing content and BTS, not a tutorial channel.

## Seven title systems (from vidIQ)

1. Price Myth Tests
2. The $12 to $200 Brand Flip
3. Marketing Autopsy (with a famous/recognizable brand)
4. Ad Lab (same product, 3 ads, real money spent)
5. Game vs Reality
6. Cinematic Set Piece / Movie Recreation
7. Access + On Set

Two starting pillars:
- **Pillar A — Brand Flip / Ad Lab**: shows skill, drives client DMs.
- **Pillar B — Cheap vs Expensive / Game vs Reality**: drives raw views.

Recommended shoot structure: film both pillars in one day. Price myth test at the range in the morning, cinematic commercial for the winning product in the afternoon.

## How this maps to the five chapters (see hooks.md)

The five chapters (Test, Teardown, Rebuild, Job, Film) are the *filming format*. The seven title systems are the *title/thumbnail wrapper*. Rough mapping:
- The Test — Price Myth Tests, Game vs Reality
- The Teardown — Marketing Autopsy
- The Rebuild — The $12 to $200 Brand Flip, Ad Lab
- The Job — Access + On Set
- The Film — Cinematic Set Piece

Use the chapter to decide what to film, use the matching title system to decide what to call it and what the thumbnail shows.

## Title/thumbnail rules, by chapter

- **Test**: title is a versus or a number ("$400 AR vs 2011", "How Many Mags Does It Take To..."). Thumbnail is a split frame or a pile of the thing with a number on it.
- **Teardown**: the brand's own logo or product does the thumbnail work — don't compete with it, frame around it. Title names the brand.
- **Rebuild**: thumbnail is the ugly/before product in hand. Title implies the transformation ("I rebranded a company that didn't ask me to").
- **Job**: thumbnail is an on-set moment, not a talking head. Title promises access ("What a $2,500 shoot actually looks like").
- **Film**: thumbnail is the cinematic hero shot. Title can be sparse — the visual carries it.

General rule carried over from the hooks work: if the underlying idea is pure marketing-thinking with no physical anchor, fix the anchor before trying to fix the title — no title formula rescues a thumbnail of someone sitting at a desk.

## Keyword data (Sept 2026 snapshot — refresh periodically via vidIQ, in a live session, never automated)

- "cheap vs expensive" ~98K/mo — highest opportunity found so far
- "cinematic commercial" ~9K/mo
- "marketing breakdown" ~9.8K/mo, but only when paired with a famous brand name
- Zero volume: "firearm marketing", "brand transformation", "rebrand fail" / "marketing fail" — avoid these as literal title language
