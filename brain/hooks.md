## Core principles

- **Open loops**: open a curiosity loop in the first line, keep it open as long as possible. When one loop closes, open the next immediately. This is the backbone of retention.
- **Scroll-back effect**: people swipe on autopilot. The goal isn't just stopping the scroll, it's making them swipe *back*. That reversal is a strong algorithm signal.
- **Context first**: the biggest mistake is jumping into content without establishing why the viewer should care in the first frame. Never assume they know what's happening.
- **Safe zones**: keep text off platform UI elements (comment box, profile info).
- **Profile-to-content match**: what someone sees on your profile after clicking through has to match the hook's promise, or it kills trust and conversion.

## Curated picks from the 1000 Viral Hooks, by category

Best patterns per category, condensed (links stripped — full list with IG examples is in `brain/sources/1000-viral-hooks.pdf`).

**Educational**
- "This represents your X before, during, and after X."
- "Here's exactly how much [action/item] you need to [result]."
- "This is what [thing] looks like when you're [action]. This is what it looks like when you're not."
- "It took me 10 years to learn this but I'll teach it to you in less than a minute."
- "If I woke up [pain point] tomorrow and wanted [dream result] by [time], here's exactly what I'd do."
- "Everyone tells you to [action] but nobody tells you how. Here's a step-by-step you can save."
- "If I had to start over, here are the top [#] things I'd do to [dream result]."
- "In 60 seconds I'm going to teach you more about [thing] than you've ever learned."
- "These are the 3 most common mistakes I see people make with [noun]."

**Comparison**
- "This is an [X], and this is a [Y]."
- "Cheap vs. expensive [noun]."
- "This [option 1] has [thing] in it, and this [option 2] has [thing] in it."
- "This group did [action], this group didn't. Here's what happened."
- "Both groups gained the same [result]. This group did [X], this group did [Y]."
- "This is [noun] before [action]. This is [noun] after [action]."

**Myth Busting**
- "Stop using [item] for [result]."
- "This is why doing [action] makes you [pain point]."
- "You are not [bad label], you just haven't [solution] yet."
- "They said '[cliché/quote].' That's a lie."
- "Just because you do [action] doesn't make you a good [label]."
- "You're using your [noun] wrong, and I'm going to show you the right way."
- "I said it before and I'll say it again: [mind-blowing fact]."

**Storytelling**
- "X years ago I decided to [decision]."
- "X years ago I was [action] because I [pain point]."
- "In [time], I went from [before state] to [after state]."
- "This is the story of how I managed to [achievement]."
- "I don't have a backup plan, so this kind of needs to work."
- "X days/months/years ago I quit [bad habit]."
- "I started [business] when I was [age] with [$]."

**Authority**
- "My [before state] used to look like this, now it looks like this."
- "[#] years it took me from [before state] to [after state]."
- "Just [#] [action] took my client from [before] to [after]."
- "I got [dream result] with minimal [action], here's how."
- "How I got my [noun] from this to this, completely natural."
- "I have never ended [period] with [result] or below."

**Day in the Life**
- "Day in the life of a [role]."
- "Come to work with me as a [title]."
- "This is what an average day looks like a week out from [event]."
- "We all have the same 24 hours in a day, so here's how I put mine to work."

**Random / Pattern-interrupt**
- "This is [large number] of [item]."
- "[Big brand] didn't want to sponsor this video — let me show you what they're missing."
- "What [title] says vs. what they mean."
- "I do not believe in [common belief], I believe in [your belief]."

## Hook Pacing Method (content multiplication)

One pillar piece → 7+ micro-topics from six angles on the same core idea:
1. Systems & Processes (educational)
2. Stories & Examples (storytelling)
3. Mistakes & Misconceptions (myth busting)
4. Questions & Concerns (educational, FAQ-style)
5. Metaphors & Analogies (comparison)
6. Realizations & Insights (authority)
7. Systems Deep Dive (step-by-step, "if I started over")

Script skeleton for any of these:
```
HOOK (0-3s): chosen hook formula
PROMISE (3-5s): "By the end you'll know how to [benefit]"
PROBLEM (10-15s): why this matters, the pain point
PROCESS (30-45s): the steps
PROOF (5-10s): the result, yours or a client's
CTA (3-5s): what to do next
```

Data point from the tracked-performance frameworks (not channel-specific yet): authority and storytelling hooks outperformed pure educational hooks; before/after structure outperformed straight tips by roughly 2x. Treat as a starting prior, replace with real channel data once it exists.

## Channel-specific packaging: the five chapters

The recurring formats these videos get filmed under, established Aug 2026. All content is one underlying idea — "looking at a product/brand and revealing how the marketing works" — expressed at five difficulty/access levels. The packaging problem for marketing-thinking content is that it has no natural image; each chapter forces a physical or visible anchor so it's clickable.

**1. The Test** — two things enter, one wins, or one question with a countable answer. Title is a versus or a number. Thumbnail is a split frame or a pile with a digit on it. Easiest to package, only chapter needing zero special access.

**2. The Teardown** — a real company's marketing, why it worked or didn't. The brand's own logo does the thumbnail work. Fixed setup: screen recording plus the product on the table.

**3. The Rebuild** — take a real product, remake its marketing on camera. Ugly product in hand for the thumbnail, finished ad as the payoff. Best format for pulling clients — an unsolicited audition, and the company doesn't get to keep the work.

**4. The Job** — BTS of a real client shoot day. Unfair advantage: shoot days are already happening.

**5. The Film** — flagship cinematic pieces (Tier1, SWAT gas mask). Rare, not a repeatable rhythm.

Rule: if a video idea can't be anchored to a physical object or visible outcome, it will be hard to package no matter how good the marketing insight is. Fix the anchor before fixing the title.
